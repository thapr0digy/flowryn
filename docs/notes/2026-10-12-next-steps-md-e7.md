# Bounded Herdr subscription and contract-check stop

- Status: done
- Open: not run live against a real Herdr or a real harness (the fake Herdr and the contract fakes only)
- Config: `[herdr] handshake_ms`

`docs/next_steps.md` package 6, first slice: give the two long-lived external operations it names, the Herdr event subscription and the daemon's automatic contract check, one deadline and one termination owner each.

## Design

### 1. The Herdr subscription handshake

**Boundary.** `HerdrClient.call` (`src/runtime/herdr/client.ts`) rejects with `timeout` after 15 s. `subscribe` has no deadline: a peer that accepts the connection and never answers leaves the promise pending for ever, so `HerdrWatcher.connect` never retries, never calls `onDown`, and no outage episode opens. The handshake also takes *any* JSON line without `error` as the acknowledgement (an event line, `{}`, a reply to another id), and skips non-JSON lines both before and after it. After the handshake a malformed line is dropped in silence; since an event only names a pane to re-read (M0 h01), a dropped line is a missed observation that nothing makes up for.

**Rule.**

- The handshake has a deadline, `[herdr] handshake_ms` (default 15 000, the ordinary request deadline), counted from the start of `subscribe`, so a hung connect is covered too. Past it, the attempt rejects with `HerdrError("timeout")` and the socket is destroyed.
- The acknowledgement is valid only as the first non-blank line, a JSON object with `id` equal to the request's id and `result.type === "subscription_started"` (the shape the h01 spike read, `.result.type`, and the fake serves). `{error}` keeps Herdr's own code. Anything else (not JSON, another id, another type, an over-long line) rejects with `protocol`. A socket error or close before it is `unreachable` when the socket never connected and `protocol` after.
- After the acknowledgement, a line that is not a JSON object with a string `event` (and an object `data` when present) closes the subscription with `protocol`: unknown evidence is reported, not discarded. It goes through the existing path: `onClose(error)` → `HerdrWatcher.retry` (backoff, `onDown` → the outage episode and its P1 rule) → a full reconcile pass on reconnect, which re-reads every run.
- The same rule for the evidence a pane read gives: `pane.process_info` with no `process_info` object, a `shell_pid` that is not a positive integer, a `foreground_process_group_id` that is not a non-negative integer, or a `foreground_processes` that is not an array of entries with positive integer `pid`, is `HerdrError("protocol")`, never an empty process list. The reconciler already reads a `HerdrError` as liveness `unknown`; before this, a malformed reply read as "pane exists, harness not in it" and could raise an `orphaned` divergence, and `ensurePane` could reuse a pane whose `0 === 0` group looked idle.

**Files.** `src/runtime/herdr/client.ts`, `src/runtime/herdr/runtime.ts`, `src/config/workers.ts` (the key), `src/daemon/daemon.ts` (passes it to both `HerdrClient`s), `test/helpers/fake-herdr.ts` (a subscribe mode: `answer`, `silent`, or a raw first line), tests in `test/unit/runtime.test.ts` and `test/integration/outages.test.ts`.

`[herdr] handshake_ms` is read when the daemon makes its Herdr client (at start, or when it finds a Herdr that started after it); a reload changes it from the next client on.

### 2. The automatic contract check's stop

**Boundary.** `spawnAutoCheck` (`src/contracts/auto.ts`) SIGKILLs the `doctor --contracts --for-daemon` child at `AUTO_MAX_RUN_MS`, so the runner's cleanup (`src/contracts/runner.ts`) never runs: its scratch daemon, Herdr workspace, harness process and `fwc-*` scratch home stay. `stop()` gives the child 10 s after SIGTERM and then SIGKILLs it, while one cleanup step alone may take longer (the cancel call's 60 s reply wait, the scratch daemon's 15 s + 5 s stop, a harness's 10 s grace, the scratch home's 10 s removal). The child is in the daemon's process group, and nothing records which resources a killed child owned. The outcome mixes the two questions: a check is "passed" or "failed", and whether it left anything behind is not recorded at all.

**Rule.**

- *Termination owner.* The `AutoContractChecker` owns the child end to end; the child (`runContract`) owns its scratch resources and writes what it owns to an ownership manifest whose path the checker gives it (`FLOWRYN_CONTRACT_MANIFEST`, read only with `--for-daemon`). The manifest (JSON, rewritten atomically) holds the runner's own process identity, the scratch home, the Herdr socket, the branch prefix and project, then the scratch daemon's identity once spawned, and at the end the cleanup outcome. The exact workspace ids and harness identities come from the scratch database inside the scratch home (as the runner's own cleanup reads them), and the manifest copies them in when cleanup starts.
- *Budget.* Every runner cleanup step gets an explicit bound (cancel call 15 s, scratch daemon 15 s + 5 s, each workspace close 5 s, each harness 10 s + 2 s, worktrees by `git`, scratch home 10 s), and their sum is `CONTRACT_CLEANUP_BUDGET_MS`, exported from `runner.ts`. The runner's signal handler also bounds itself: a cleanup that has not finished by the budget writes `cleanup: {ok: false}` and exits. A repeated SIGTERM while cleaning is ignored rather than killing the runner mid-cleanup. The handler is installed as soon as the scratch home exists, so a SIGTERM during launch is handled too.
- *Escalation.* The child runs in its own process group (`detached`). On the check's deadline the checker sends SIGTERM, waits `CONTRACT_CLEANUP_BUDGET_MS` plus a 10 s margin, then SIGKILLs the group (the runner and its scratch daemon, never the Flowryn daemon), so the end-to-end budget of a check is `AUTO_MAX_RUN_MS + stop grace + 5 s`. On a daemon stop the checker sends SIGTERM and waits at most the existing 10 s (inside launchd's 20 s exit timeout); a child still cleaning is left to finish its own bounded cleanup, being in its own group, and the next start handles whatever remains (below).
- *Recovery.* After a child ends, the checker reads the manifest. A missing or failed cleanup outcome runs `recoverOwned` (`src/contracts/owned.ts`) once: a still-living runner (by identity, never by pid alone) is stopped (SIGTERM, grace, SIGKILL of its group); then the scratch daemon and harness identities are terminated, each workspace id is closed in the recorded Herdr socket, own worktrees are removed by the existing prefix rule (`removeOwnWorktrees`), and the scratch home is removed only when it is a `fwc-*` directory holding the scratch `config.toml`. At daemon start, an attempt a stop cut off (`running`, with a manifest) gets the same recovery before any new check starts, and so does a `waiting` attempt that still names a manifest (an earlier start's release did not finish; the manifest is dropped from the attempt only once settled). Nothing is touched by label, and nothing outside the manifest's exact ids: unrelated workspaces, worktrees and sessions stay.
- *Two outcomes.* `AutoAttempt` keeps `state` for the behavioural result (passed, failed, unrecorded; a timed-out check is still failed) and gains `cleanup` (`ok`, `failed`, or `none` when the run kept no manifest, so it recorded nothing it owned), `cleanup_detail` and `manifest`. A cleanup that recovery could not finish appends `contract.auto_cleanup_failed` (by, problems, manifest) and opens one P1 `contract_leftover` naming what is left and the manifest; a clean or recovered one appends `contract.auto_cleanup` (`ok`, `by: runner|daemon`; `ok: null` for `none`). A pass with a failed cleanup is still recorded as a pass; a failure with a clean cleanup is still a failure. The daemon reads the runner's cleanup from the manifest, not from the child's stdout, since a killed child prints nothing. `ContractResult` gains `cleanup` (so the report file has it), and `doctor --contracts` shows a `contract <harness> cleanup` check, a warning when something was left, separate from the suite's own checks and from doctor's exit code.

**Files.** `src/contracts/auto.ts`, `src/contracts/runner.ts`, new `src/contracts/owned.ts`, `src/contracts/cleanup.ts` (`removeOwnWorktrees` returns what it could not remove), `src/cli/commands/setup.ts` (manifest env, the cleanup check), `src/lifecycle/harness-gate.ts` (`AutoAttempt` fields), `src/attention/items.ts` (`contract_leftover`), `src/runtime/herdr/runtime.ts` (`closeWorkspace` takes a deadline).

**Tests.**

- `test/unit/auto-contract.test.ts`: the stop grace covers the cleanup budget (68.5 s); past the deadline a child that ignores SIGTERM gets SIGKILL only after the grace, fails, and has its manifest released; one that stops within the grace is never killed; a pass that left something is recorded with one `contract_leftover` P1, and a failure with a clean cleanup opens none; a runner's clean cleanup is taken at its word; no manifest is `none`; a daemon stop never kills the child, and the next start releases its manifest before any new check, even when a start between them was cut off mid-release; an attempt from before manifests reruns with nothing released.
- `test/unit/contract-owned.test.ts`: `recoverOwned` against real processes, a real scratch home and the fake Herdr ends exactly the manifest's and scratch database's processes and workspace and removes the home, while an unrelated process, workspace and `fwc-*` directory stay; an unreachable Herdr is an explicit failure naming the workspace; a missing, unreadable or thin manifest is unknown ownership; only a real `fwc-*` directory is removed.
- `test/integration/auto-contract.test.ts`: the compiled daemon's check, stopped mid-suite by SIGKILL of the runner's group (the daemon releases it) and by SIGTERM (the runner releases it itself): no live pane, the scratch daemon gone, the scratch home gone, the unrelated pane still there, `contract.auto_failed` and `contract.auto_cleanup` recorded apart.
- `test/integration/contracts-offline.test.ts`: the `contract <harness> cleanup` check is ok.
- `test/unit/runtime.test.ts`, `test/integration/outages.test.ts`, `test/unit/config.test.ts`: the handshake deadline, the acknowledgement and event-line rules, `pane.process_info` validation, a silent and a malformed peer through the daemon's outage path, and the key's validation. Each of the handshake cases failed before the fix (a silent peer kept the daemon's startup pass from ever running).

**Migration.** None: `AutoAttempt` lives as JSON in `meta`, and the new fields are optional; an attempt from before this change has no manifest and is treated as before (rerun, no recovery).

**Out of scope.** Other long-lived operations (verification steps, no-mistakes, Docker), Herdr reply validation beyond the subscription and `pane.process_info`, and changing launchd's exit timeout. The cleanup guard (`scripts/guard/`) is not touched; both of its fixture sets pass.
